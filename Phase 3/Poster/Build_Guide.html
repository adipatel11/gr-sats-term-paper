Part 4. Motivation and Research Question

  1. Foundation models are adapted to skin lesion images with linear probing (LP) or LoRA. A doctor may use the predicted risk to decide on a biopsy, so 20% of the lesions given a 20% risk should be malignant (calibration). The risk is usually calibrated on clean images, but real photos can be blurry, noisy, compressed, or badly lit, and calibration also differs between patient groups.

    WidthHeightHorizontalVertical
    11.1"1.85"0.6"3.6"

  2. Research question: Can a post-hoc calibrator that sees only the image at test time keep the worst-off patient group calibrated under corruptions it was not fit on, and how does the answer depend on how the model was adapted?

    Objective: build a calibrator fit for the worst group and test when it helps.

Part 8. Proposed Methodology: GR-SATS

  1. WidthHeightHorizontalVertical
    11.1"4"12.45"3.65"

  2. Fig. 3. The GR-SATS workflow. At test time it needs only the image and the adapted model.

    WidthHeightHorizontalVertical
    11.1"0.36"12.45"7.63"
  3. WidthHeightHorizontalVertical
    11.1"0.85"12.45"7.97"
    • C1, shift score: log distance to the 10th nearest clean training image, standardized and clipped so one design works for every backbone and adaptation.
    • C2, calibrator: the inverse temperature is linear in the score and stays positive, so predictions at 0.5 never flip. a₁ = 0 gives ordinary TS [10].
    • C3, worst-group fit: picks a₀ and a₁ to minimize the largest group loss, as in GroupDRO [11]. Convex, solved with SLSQP in seconds to minutes.
    • C4, DAC-WG: DAC fit with the same worst-group loss, to separate the effect of the objective from the form of the calibrator.

Part 9. Experimental Setup

    • 8 settings, 3 seeds: 2 datasets x 2 backbones (DINOv2 [12], BiomedCLIP [13]) x 2 adaptations (LP, rank-8 LoRA [14]).
    • Baselines: no calibration, TS fit on clean images, Gaussian noise [15], or corruptions, DAC, AAR, multi-domain TS, and cluster-wise TS.
    • Metrics: worst-group (WG) negative log-likelihood (NLL) and Brier score. Also ECE, AUC.
    • Validation: 5-fold cross-fitting with one corruption family held out. Tests use unseen images and an unseen family at severities 3 to 5.
    • Statistics: paired bootstrap (2,000 draws), Friedman and Nemenyi tests.
    • Implementation: PyTorch and SciPy on free Google Colab T4 GPUs.

Part 12. Discussion and Limitations

    • Answer: partly yes. GR-SATS matches an oracle that knows each patient's group (0.398), but WG NLL is still higher than on clean images.
    • Adaptation matters. The gain over TS corruptions is 0.067 and 0.076 on HAM with LP, but 0.027 and 0.024 with LoRA.
    • DAC-WG ties (0.397), so the gain over DAC comes from the objective.
    • Costs: most of the gain is on noise (0.044 falls to 0.009 without it). Clean ECE is 0.028 against 0.013 for clean TS, so that criterion failed. Mean risk for patients under 60 is 11.4% when 5.1% are malignant.
    • Not run: official DAC and AAR code (both reimplemented), TS with textures, ImageNet ViT, full fine-tuning, a from-scratch rerun.

Part 13. Conclusions and Future Work

  1. Conclusions:

    • GR-SATS lowers WG NLL by 0.044 against TS corruptions, 0.013 against DAC, and 0.022 against AAR. The worst-group objective is the part that matters.
    • Calibrators should be tested on corrupted images for each group before use.

    Future work:

    • Real hospital and camera shifts, full multi-layer DAC, a shift score that still helps after LoRA, and a fit that limits the cost to other groups.
